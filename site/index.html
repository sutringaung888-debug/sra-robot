SRA Robot · v2.0 · Open source

01 How it works

Thin body. Big brain.

The ESP32-S3 is the body: microphone in, speaker out. A computer you already have, running Hermes Agent, is the brain.

01 · Hear

It hears you

An INMP441 I2S microphone captures your voice. Whisper tiny turns that speech into text on your machine, in English, at 16 kHz.

Local speech-to-text

02 · Think

Hermes decides

In the voice build, the board sends audio over Wi-Fi. Hermes calls GLM-5.2. The key is read from your Hermes config or OLLAMA_API_KEY. It is never hard-coded.

Ollama Cloud free tier

03 · Talk

It answers aloud

A MAX98357 amplifier drives a 4Ω 3W speaker. Edge TTS speaks the reply in the Andrew voice.

en-US Andrew

04 · Learn

Praise sticks

Say “good boy” and that behavior’s score goes up. Say “no” and it goes down. Scores live in a JSON log, and higher scores are preferred next time.

Reward log

The hear → think → speak pipeline, the four personalities, reward training, and Telegram health monitoring are in the repository. The physical ESP32-S3 voice body is the piece still on the bench: parts have arrived, and assembly is in progress.

02 Features

What the project already does.

These are the pieces checked off in the repository. Seeing and walking are on the roadmap, not in this list.

Hears you

INMP441 microphone plus local Whisper. The S3’s two I2S buses let the mic and the speaker run at the same time.

Thinks for free

GLM-5.2 through Ollama Cloud’s free tier. No OpenAI subscription. Hermes is the agent that calls the model.

Talks back

MAX98357 amp, 4Ω 3W speaker, Edge TTS, Andrew voice. The amp’s speaker leads screw in. Nothing is soldered.

Four personalities

Guard Dog, Playful Puppy, Smart Assistant, and Sassy Companion. It starts as the Playful Puppy. Say “be a puppy,” “guard mode,” “be professional,” or “be sassy.”

Learns from you

A reward log. Phrases like “good boy” raise a score. “No,” “stop,” and similar corrections lower it. The robot prefers the higher scores.

Reachable on Telegram

Bots for robot control and health alerts. A debug monitor watches the brain and can restart it when something fails.

Why this board

04 Build

Four steps to a talking body.

Overview of the illustrated guide. The step 1 sketch is in the repo. Steps 2–4 are specified in the guide while the voice body is still being assembled.

  1. Step 1 of 4

    Bring the brain online

    Goal: the board answers over USB. No wiring yet.

    Parts: one Hosyond ESP32-S3, a USB-C data cable, and your computer.

    You should see the power LED, then the serial monitor at 115200 baud printing “SRA Robot Brain Online” and “alive 0”, “alive 1”… once a second. That sketch is esp32/step1_hello. If the computer doesn’t see the board, hold BOOT while plugging it in.

  2. Step 2 of 4

    Give it a mouth

    Goal: the first sound from the speaker.

    Parts: MAX98357 amp, 4Ω 3W speaker, and jumper wires. 5V goes to VIN, GND to GND, GPIO15 to BCLK, GPIO16 to LRC, and GPIO7 to DIN. Leave the amp’s SD gain pin unconnected. Strip the speaker leads and screw them in.

    You should hear a rising two-tone beep every 2 seconds.

  3. Step 3 of 4

    Give it ears

    Goal: say a word and hear it played back.

    Parts: the INMP441 and five jumpers. The amp stays wired. 3.3V to VDD — never 5V — GND to GND, GND to L/R for left-channel mode, GPIO4 to SCK, GPIO5 to WS, and GPIO6 to SD.

    The mic is on I2S0 and the amp is on I2S1. Face the mic away from the speaker and keep those wires short.

  4. Step 4 of 4

    Make it a robot

    Goal: breadboard the parts, then reach Hermes over Wi-Fi.

    Same pin map as steps 2 and 3. Power from the breadboard rails, not a daisy chain. The guide’s target moment is “Hey robot”: audio goes to Hermes on your computer, GLM thinks, and the speaker answers in Andrew’s voice.

    USB can stay plugged in for debugging. Wi-Fi and USB work together.

Rules that keep the parts alive

  • The INMP441 is 3.3V only. 5V destroys it.
  • The MAX98357 can take 5V, which is louder, or 3.3V, which is quieter.
  • Unplug USB before you change a wire.
  • Hold BOOT while plugging in only if the computer does not see the board.

Pin map

Mic · I2S0 · INMP441

  • 3V3 → VDD
  • GND → GND and L/R
  • GPIO4 → SCK
  • GPIO5 → WS
  • GPIO6 → SD

Amp · I2S1 · MAX98357

  • 5V → VIN
  • GND → GND
  • GPIO15 → BCLK
  • GPIO16 → LRC
  • GPIO7 → DIN
  • SD pin left unconnected

Full drawings, wire colors, and troubleshooting live in esp32/BUILD_GUIDE.html.

The brain, on the computer

install.sh is the Raspberry Pi OS setup script in the repo. The README quick start is:

git clone https://github.com/sutringaung888-debug/sra-robot.git
cd sra-robot
./install.sh
python3 robot_brain.py

Set the LLM endpoint in config.json. The key comes from your Hermes config or OLLAMA_API_KEY.

Later · not in the $65

The dog body comes after the voice body.

BUILD_PLAN.md is a second phase: a SunFounder PiDog with Hermes on a Raspberry Pi 5. No soldering iron, no 3D printer, no wire strippers. A small Phillips screwdriver is included. The plan says no robotics experience is required, and it lists assembly at 1–2 hours. The README still marks this body, and vision, as not done.

In the PiDog box

  • 12-servo body that snaps together

Assembly overview

  1. Snap the body together
  2. Insert the Pi 5
  3. Connect the camera ribbon
  4. Connect the servo cables to the Pi’s GPIO
  5. Insert a microSD card with Pi OS
  6. Connect the battery
  7. Power on

Software the plan calls for

  • Hermes Agent on the Pi 5
  • GLM-5.2 via Ollama Cloud
  • PiDog Python library for the 12 servos
  • Whisper tiny and Edge TTS
  • OpenCV and MediaPipe for vision
  • A Hermes Telegram gateway
  • OpenClaw, which the plan says the PiDog kit supports
